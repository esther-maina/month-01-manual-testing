# Define QA

- QA is a product-focused, preventive process that systematically challenges assumptions in software to catch defects early and reduce the risk of shipping broken features to users.

## Where QA sits in a team

In a well-run team QA is embedded sitting inside the team consisting of PM, Engineering manager, developers & designers

## Difference between PM and Engineering Manager

- Product Manager: Decides what get built and why, owns the roadmap and requirements. Not technical role.

- Engineering Manager: Decides how it gets built. Manages developers and QA. Technical lead.

## The Phone Analogy

- Assume you want to order food online. You reach out to your phone, open the app and make an order.  Then you will wait, when the delivery guy arrives he going to reach out to you through the same phone you used if the SIM card is still in the same phone. That's where the modern well-run team, a QA will sit together with the technical team.

## Difference between siloed and embedded QA

- Siloed QA: A separate QA team that sits outside the development squad, reporting to a QA Manager. Code is handed to them at the end of development. QA has less influence and bugs are found too late.

- Embedded QA: Sits in the table as technical team, Same standup, same sprint, same PM. Their voice carries the same weight as a developer's.

## How does a QA handles disagreements with a developer?

- QA and Developer collaborate together they listen to another, as a QA I would come with an evidence assume it about a bug that affects UX. I would say: "This is what user experience when they try to click this button to repay their payment and has to call support team."
